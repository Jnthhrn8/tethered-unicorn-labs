From a clue to a reviewable case.

  1. Start with what you know. Describe the topic, useful clues, and what would count as an answer.
  2. Choose a specialist. Each has a stable mission, voice, and approach to follow-up questions.
  3. Search and review. Public-source searches and page captures help assemble the evidence. Missing or inaccessible sources remain limitations.
  4. Refine the question. Follow relevant clues, separate competing possibilities, and distinguish facts from inferences.
  5. Organize the findings. Keep research in cases with reports and supporting evidence that a person can revisit.

Six personalities. One respect for evidence.

These are AI specialist working styles in the Lantern Company, not a claim of six human experts. Their fictional character shapes how they investigate and explain; it does not replace source verification. Meet the full Company.

Public people-search leads

Marelowe

A patient investigator with a velvet voice and an allergy to mistaken identity.

Separates candidate profiles, follows public clues, and asks the question most likely to distinguish similar matches. A lead is never proof of identity.

General research and reports

Quill

A curious research librarian who loves the footnotes almost as much as the story.

Explores the landscape of a topic, connects findings, and assembles explanations with sources. Clarifies scope when the evidence reveals meaningful choices.

Technical research

Rivet

A meticulous workshop engineer: direct, exact, and delighted by a good datasheet.

Looks for specifications, repositories, identifiers, exact versions, and primary documentation. Useful when a similar-looking part or outdated manual would change the answer.

Legal-source research

Blackletter

A sober law librarian with dry wit and strict jurisdictional manners.

Locates and maps legal authority by jurisdiction, court, and date. Research support, not legal advice; citations and applicability still need qualified review.

Property and public records

Deedee

A friendly title-room veteran who follows the paper trail.

Explores parcels, deeds, assessments, permits, and property history. Tries address, parcel, county, and recorder clues while leaving gaps clearly marked.

Shopping and offer comparisons

Glimmer

A cheerful bargain scout with a jeweler’s eye for fine print.

Compares specifications, delivered cost, timing, seller risk, and discounts. Flags unverified stock, checkout totals, coupon eligibility, and delivery claims.

A little sparkle. No invented certainty.

The shared specialist principles call for separating verified facts, reasonable inferences, and open questions; preserving source provenance; and saying plainly when the evidence cannot answer a question.

  • Public-source research is not access to private accounts or restricted databases.
  • Names, records, offers, and summaries can be incomplete, stale, or misleading. Review the original source.
  • People-search matches are leads, not identity proof. Use research responsibly and respect privacy.
  • Legal and property findings support research; they are not professional legal advice or a certified title search.
  • Shopping comparisons reflect available evidence. Verify final terms with the seller.

What is growing next?

A curated knowledge layer and durable specialist definitions are under development to improve reuse of relevant sources and maintain consistent working styles. This is ongoing work, not a claim that every future retrieval feature is already available in the preview.
